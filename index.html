01 / About

A little but not so little about me

Born and raised in Naharlagun,a small town nestled in the northeasternmost state of India,Arunachal Pradesh,where the sun makes its first appearance over the country,I grew up with rather limited exposure to computer science and technology in general.Mathematics, however, was an early fascination.It was only in high school that I first encountered Python and SQL,and the prospect of translating mathematical ideas into code gradually took hold. Computer Science seemed a rather natural choice for university, being,to my mind, one of the most direct ways of putting mathematics to work.

I like working around problems concerning humans and solving them using AI.AI for humans, or humans for AI? Both ways, actually.I'm interested in human-centred intelligent systems particularly in social and cultural contexts.Currently I’m working on an audio-based lexical retrieval system for Apatani,an indigenous dialect of Arunachal Pradesh. The project focuses on using speech representations and contextual information to retrieve words and expressions from complete, partial, or ambiguous speech, without relying on standardised romanised input since the dialect doesn’t have a agreed upon script.I have also been exploring the use of deep generative models to document, classify, and generate traditional handloom motifs of indigenous communities in Arunachal Pradesh. The broader idea is to investigate how computer vision and generative modelling might contribute to documenting and reinterpreting indigenous visual traditions. Although the project began some time ago, it remains in its innate stage, largely because assembling a sufficiently extensive and well-documented dataset has proved more challenging than initially anticipated. Much of our current effort is therefore directed towards collecting additional samples and documenting the motifs, while ensuring that the cultural context and narratives associated with them are not lost in the process.

Of late, I've also worked around representation learning, more specifically on model aggregation,alignment, and fusion. My recent research investigates the application of Optimal Transport theory to fusing independently trained Transformers with heterogeneous depths.The central idea is to establish structural and parameter-level correspondences between networks across different scales, using Fused Gromov–Wasserstein transport, Bures–Wasserstein geometry, and entropic Sinkhorn transport. An interesting aspect of this work has been examining the assumptions underlying network alignment and understanding how different alignment strategies influence the eventual fusion of neural representations. The project originated during my research internship at National Chung Cheng University (CCU), Taiwan, under the Taiwan Experience Education Program (TEEP) in the summer of 2026.We are hopeful to publish our work early next year.

Apart from all this, I find joy in cooking, making kimchi during gimjang and hosting lunches for people.I also write occasionally, and some of my ramblings find their way onto Medium.Although I loved reading through my teenage years, that habit has transcended more into writing now : mostly random thoughts mixed with nostalgic fragments that occur to me at times.

Outside all this, I'm always up for a conversation about post-war history, the origin stories behind our food , and aviation, preferably over a cup of Oolong tea. Read on Medium: Hiraeth, A Home in Arunachal ↗

02 / Research

Research experience

National Chung Cheng University, Taiwan Research Intern
Jun 2026 – Aug 2026
TEEP · Chiayi Most recent

Heterogeneous transformer fusion along statistical manifolds: a Bures–Wasserstein approach

Dept. of Computer Science and Information Engineering. The work develops a multi-scale fusion pipeline and, more importantly, examines which component actually drives its performance. Through an analytical study of Wasserstein barycentric interpolation under the Bures metric, I show that the fused weights depend on distributional statistics that are invariant to row permutations, meaning permutation alignment is not the mechanism responsible for the observed gains.

Experiments across multiple alignment settings support this: hard permutation alignment is statistically indistinguishable from no alignment, while replacing Bures interpolation with Euclidean interpolation substantially degrades zero-shot fusion performance. The benefit of optimal-transport-based fusion, in this setting, comes from distributional matching of weight statistics rather than resolving permutation symmetry.

Optimal Transport Fused Gromov–Wasserstein Sinkhorn Vision Transformers
IIT Mandi Summer Research Intern
Jun 2025 – Jul 2025
Mandi, HP

Drone surveillance & trajectory prediction

Under Dr Dinesh Singh, I worked on two problems at the intersection of optimisation and machine learning. First: scheduling drone patrols to minimise flight time under patrol-interval constraints — ILP, Greedy, Simulated Annealing and a novel multi-stage method, tested on real campus maps (Ritsumeikan University, IISER Bhopal) for high coverage at reduced runtime. Second: the Trajectron model for multi-agent trajectory prediction using CVAEs and dynamic spatiotemporal graphs, evaluated on ETH/UCY with competitive ADE/FDE/NLL and outperforming baselines such as SGAN.

PyTorch ILP CVAEs Simulated Annealing Spatiotemporal Graphs

What I've been working on

Deep generative systems for indigenous textile archiving

Applying deep learning to digitise, classify and re-envision the indigenous handloom motifs of Arunachal Pradesh. CNN/GAN pipelines for regional motif classification and generative synthesis, a searchable dataset integrating visual data, metadata and cultural narratives, and a prototype AI-assisted pattern-design toolkit using neural style transfer.

CNN · GAN · Style Transfer
CCU Taiwan Jun 2026 – Oct 2026

Heterogeneous transformer fusion along statistical manifolds: a Bures–Wasserstein approach

Fusing heterogeneous vision transformers by formulating model fusion as a computational graph alignment problem. Fused Gromov–Wasserstein transport handles the macro structure, Wasserstein barycentric synthesis resolves the depth mismatch without heuristic identity padding, and Sinkhorn alignment handles parameters.

Optimal Transport · Model Fusion
IISER Bhopal Aug 2026 – Ongoing

Federated learning for heterogeneous Indian judicial data

Privacy-preserving machine learning across decentralised judicial datasets, under Dr Haroon R. Lone. Federated architectures that keep data local; non-IID distributions arising from jurisdictional differences, procedural variations and heterogeneous case characteristics; and aggregation strategies weighed on predictive performance, client-level fairness and robustness to unreliable participants.

Federated Learning · Non-IID
Independent Oct 2026 – Ongoing

Beyond acoustic matching: spoken lexical retrieval for Apatani

An audio-first, orthography-independent retrieval framework for Apatani, an indigenous language of Arunachal Pradesh. It identifies words from complete, partial and contextually ambiguous speech without romanised input, ranking candidates on contextual evidence rather than acoustic similarity alone, and introduces ApataniLex, a benchmark with native-speaker validation.

Speech · Low-resource NLP
IIT Bhubaneswar Dec 2024 – Jan 2025

Geospatial LLM inference for travel

The data backbone for an LLM-powered travel decision system: scraping flight data, curating geospatial datasets for Indian destinations, integrating APIs. The kind of infrastructure work that makes “AI-powered” tools actually function.
