Step 01준비Done

First, get everything on the board

Where I come from, and what I'm actually working on.

Growing up in Naharlagun, a small town in Arunachal Pradesh, I didn't have the typical CS kid story. No bootcamps at twelve, no startup garage. What I had was curiosity — about how things work, why people choose what they choose, and what happens when you put the right spices in at the wrong moment.

That curiosity led me to IISER Bhopal and, eventually, to a fairly specific corner of machine learning: deep generative models and representation learning. What a model learns is rarely the thing you asked it to learn, and almost never in a form that lines up with another model's. Most of my work is some version of that problem.

At National Chung Cheng University in Taiwan I treated the fusion of two heterogeneous Vision Transformers as a graph alignment problem and solved it with optimal transport. In an ongoing project I use a gradient reversal layer to strip demographic signal out of speech representations, so a Parkinson's classifier stops leaning on the speaker instead of the disease. In another, I train generative models on the handloom motifs of Arunachal Pradesh — a domain with almost no data and a great deal at stake in getting it right.

The kitchen metaphor isn't decoration. Cooking is where I learned that technique is only half of it: the rest is knowing what you're working with, respecting where it came from, and having the patience to let something reduce. I cook my way through a never-ending list of dishes, write nostalgic threads of memory on Medium, and read travel magazines the way other people read novels. I convened NEVISTA, IISER Bhopal's North-East festival, largely so a few hundred people could taste home at once.

Deep generative systems for indigenous textile archiving

옛 무늬, 새로 짜다yet munui, saero jjada

An independent project applying deep learning to digitise, classify and re-envision the handloom motifs of Arunachal Pradesh. CNN and GAN pipelines handle regional motif classification and generative synthesis; a searchable dataset binds the visual data to its metadata and its cultural narrative; neural style transfer folds traditional motifs into contemporary patterns.

II

Demographic-agnostic Parkinson's detection

목소리에서 사람을 지우다moksori-eseo sarameul jiuda

A fairness-aware framework that detects Parkinson's from Mel-spectrogram representations of speech. A tri-branch adversarial CNN with a Gradient Reversal Layer disentangles pathological features from demographic attributes, over an audio pipeline of VAD, STFT, Mel-scale transformation and SpecAugment.

Cross-linguistic generalisation evaluated across mPower, PC-GITA and Czech PD.
On the stove · Apr 2026Gradient reversalMel-spectrogramsSpecAugment
III

Multi-scale optimal transport for transformer fusion

두 모델, 한 그릇du model, han geureut

Fusing heterogeneous Vision Transformers by formulating model fusion as computational graph alignment. Fused Gromov–Wasserstein transport handles the macro structure, Wasserstein barycentric synthesis resolves the six-versus-twelve-layer depth mismatch without heuristic identity padding, and Sinkhorn alignment settles the parameters.

49.74% on CIFAR-100 after fifteen fine-tuning epochs — past both parent models.

Multi-scale optimal transport for heterogeneous transformer fusion

I developed a multi-scale optimal transport framework treating the fusion of heterogeneous transformers as a computational graph alignment problem — a macro-to-micro pipeline combining Fused Gromov–Wasserstein transport, Wasserstein barycentric synthesis and Sinkhorn-based parameter alignment. Rather than padding 6-layer and 12-layer Vision Transformers to a shared identity, the barycentric step resolves the architectural depth mismatch directly. On CIFAR-100 the fused model reached 49.74% test accuracy after fifteen fine-tuning epochs, outperforming both independently trained parents.
